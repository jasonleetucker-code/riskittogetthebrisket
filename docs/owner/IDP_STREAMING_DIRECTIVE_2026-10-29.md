2. THE ACTUAL PROBLEM WE ARE SOLVING

My dynasty fantasy football league includes a manager named Brent. Brent routinely keeps approximately three to five roster spots available for streaming defensive players. He acquires available defensive linemen, linebackers, and defensive backs who are expected to perform well in upcoming matchups, then replaces them when better opportunities arise. This potentially creates a competitive advantage because: lower-tier IDP dynasty values are often tightly compressed; weekly defensive production is volatile; injuries and playing-time changes create short-term opportunities; individual matchups can influence defensive statistical production; useful weekly IDP starters may remain available even in deep leagues; some low-value bench players offer little future roster utility; playoff games can make short-term lineup improvement especially valuable. However, reckless streaming can destroy dynasty value. I do not want to lose a promising 23-year-old linebacker simply to acquire a 31-year-old defender with a favorable one-week projection. Likewise, I do not want to occupy valuable roster spots indefinitely with mediocre players who have little developmental upside or meaningful future usefulness. The essential question: "When does the expected competitive benefit of acquiring a weekly IDP streamer justify releasing an existing dynasty asset?" We need Chase Upside to answer this mathematically, using actual projections, roster construction, dynasty values, developmental information, and competitive context. Importantly, we must first determine whether streaming offers a measurable advantage rather than assuming Brent's strategy is optimal.
